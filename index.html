From Sketchbook to Screen 10 short lessons

Your eye already knows more than you think.

Graphic and web design are not a rejection of drawing or painting. They are a new medium for the same visual decisions—now joined by readable type, useful structure, interaction, and screens that change size.

Keep your visual voice. Add a design system.

What transfers

Value, focal points, negative space, rhythm, colour relationships, texture, and the habit of noticing what a viewer sees first.

What changes

Design must help someone find, read, decide, and act. It also has to adapt to different screens and different access needs.

What you’ll make

A small screen-based composition each step of the way, ending in a compact poster, landing page, portfolio page, or interface concept.

Prepare a low-friction studio.

  • Bring a sketchbook or loose paper and one familiar drawing tool.
  • Choose a modest real-world subject: an event poster, art portfolio, character commission page, or small shop page.
  • Keep a browser window available; no software purchase or code experience is required for the first lessons.
  • Give each lesson 15–25 minutes, then return tomorrow for the next one.

Start with a subject you care about. This course moves your art practice toward clear, usable screen and graphic compositions. Name what you hope to design, and let that shape your capstone.

Look. Decide. Make. Check.

Each lesson gives one idea, connects it to a skill you already have, and asks you to make a small decision. Do the quick browser exercise, then apply it to your own chosen subject. The goal is durable judgment, not collecting design vocabulary.

When something feels unclear, ask your teacher a follow-up question. Better yet, show a rough sketch or screenshot and ask what is working and what to adjust.
