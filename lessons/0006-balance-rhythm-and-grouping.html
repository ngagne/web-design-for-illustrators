15–20 minutes · Composition

Make the page breathe in rhythm.

Balance is not symmetry. It is the feeling that visual weight, repetition, and pauses have been deliberately arranged.

Skill: use repetition and proximity to organize a page

Translate what you already know.

From a paintingYou balance a dark mass with a quiet open area, repeat a mark to build rhythm, and cluster objects to create a scene.
On a screenRepeated component shapes and spacing build a rhythm people can learn. Proximity tells them which labels, controls, and details belong together.

One idea

Make repetition carry the ordinary work so one intentional exception can carry emphasis. Reuse card padding, title positions, and button shapes. Then make groups visible through proximity rather than boxes around every object.

Primary source: Interaction Design Foundation’s explanation of spacing, grouping, and hierarchy.

Retrieval check

Which cue most directly says “these items belong together”?

Make a rhythm strip.

  1. Draw three repeated cards or sections for your project.
  2. Repeat their left edge, internal padding, and title treatment.
  3. Give the most important card one controlled difference: a larger image, stronger action, or more open space.
  4. Remove any border that is only compensating for weak grouping.

Ask your teacher whether the repeated parts feel like a system or like a template that has become too monotonous.
