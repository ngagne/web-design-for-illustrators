20 minutes · Type

Typography is a voice you can read.

Type is not illustration. Its personality matters—but its first job is to carry meaning with ease.

Skill: set one readable type hierarchy

Translate what you already know.

From drawingLine quality communicates: sharp, soft, playful, solemn, quiet, loud.
On a screenTypeface, size, weight, line length, leading, and spacing create a voice while determining whether a person can comfortably read.

One idea

Set type in roles: display, heading, body, and utility. Let contrast between roles do the hierarchy work. Body text needs a comfortable measure and line spacing; a typeface that is charming at poster size may exhaust a reader in a long paragraph.

W3C’s visual-presentation guidance recommends limiting text blocks to 80 characters and avoiding fully justified text. Read the primary source.

Retrieval check

Which adjustment often makes a dense body paragraph more comfortable?

Set four roles.

  1. Write a title, heading, short paragraph, and button label for your chosen project.
  2. Give the title and heading distinct scale; keep the body simple and readable.
  3. Use a second typeface only if it creates a clear role, not just because it is expressive.
  4. Read the paragraph aloud at arm’s length. If your eye loses its place, shorten the line or open the leading.

Ask your teacher to compare two font choices if you can name the mood you want but can’t decide which has the right voice.
