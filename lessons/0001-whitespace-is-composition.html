Whitespace is composition.

On a screen, empty space is a structural material. Use it to say “these belong together” and “this matters next.”

Skill: group content with spaceMake: a calmer two-part composition

Translate what you already know.

From the sketchbookYou leave breathing room around a character’s face or a bright object so it can be felt before it is named.
On a screenYou give related content a smaller internal gap and unrelated content a larger external gap, so the interface can be scanned before it is read.

One idea

Whitespace is not the absence of design. It is a visible boundary and a directional cue. A useful first rule: space within a group should be smaller than space between groups. This is visual grouping, not decoration.

Unlike a painting, a page is often read in pieces: title, action, price, description, navigation. Space tells the visitor which pieces form a thought. W3C’s design guidance explicitly recommends using headings and spacing to group related content. Read the primary source: W3C’s web-accessibility design tips.

Retrieval check

Which material separates unrelated ideas without adding new content?

Apply it on paper.

  1. Pick a tiny subject with two ideas: an art-class flyer, commission card, or character profile.
  2. Draw only rectangles and text lines—no illustrations yet.
  3. Make the gaps inside each idea visibly smaller than the gap between the ideas.
  4. Squint. If the groups merge, increase the between-group gap before adding another border or colour.

Ask your teacher a follow-up question—or share a photo of your layout—if the groups still feel ambiguous. We can diagnose the spacing together.
