20 minutes · Web-specific

Design what happens next.

Graphic design makes meaning visible. Web design also makes possibilities and consequences visible.

Skill: give one action clear affordance and feedback

Translate what you already know.

From sequential artPanels, poses, and transitions let a reader predict what has changed and what may happen next.
On a screenButtons, links, focus states, selection, errors, and confirmation let a person predict what a tap or click will do—and learn whether it worked.

One idea

An interactive object needs a visible resting state and a visible changed state. Do not rely on colour alone: use labels, icons, shape, or text so the meaning survives varied vision and contexts. Feedback should be specific: “Saved” tells more than a silent colour shift.

Primary source: W3C’s design guidance on identifiable controls and feedback.

Retrieval check

What should change after someone completes a meaningful action?

Storyboard one tap.

  1. Choose an action: book, save, submit, add, or open.
  2. Draw the resting control and write the exact label.
  3. Draw the immediate changed state after the tap.
  4. Add one concise feedback message and ask: could a first-time visitor tell what happened?

Ask your teacher to test your micro-storyboard. Describe the intended action and show only the before and after state.
