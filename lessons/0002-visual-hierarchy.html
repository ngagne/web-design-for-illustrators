15–20 minutes · Composition

Direct the eye.

Hierarchy is the planned order in which a visitor notices and understands a page.

Skill: establish a clear first, second, and third read

Translate what you already know.

From paintingA focal point earns attention through contrast, scale, edge, placement, or isolation.
On a screenThe same levers create a reading order: the visitor should see the purpose, then the important detail, then the action.

One idea

Hierarchy is not “make everything important.” It is controlled contrast between levels. Give one element the strongest claim; let the next elements support it. Size, weight, value, colour, placement, and whitespace are all levers—use only the few needed.

Primary source: Interaction Design Foundation’s visual-hierarchy guide.

Retrieval check

Which lever usually creates the clearest first read for a page title?

Make a three-read thumbnail.

  1. Pick a page or poster with one desired action.
  2. Write only: a title, one detail, and an action.
  3. Make the title the first read, the detail the second, and the action the third—using no more than two visual levers per item.
  4. Show it to someone for three seconds. Ask what they saw first.

Ask your teacher to critique your thumbnail if the order you intended is not the order people report.

Remember

Hierarchy is a sequence, not a volume knob. Make one thing loud enough, then let the rest be quiet enough.
