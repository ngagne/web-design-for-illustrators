30–60 minutes · Capstone

Make, test, revise.

Finish a small piece by treating your art instincts as hypotheses that a real viewer can confirm or challenge.

Skill: run a focused visual critique loop

Choose one compact brief.

Graphic

A poster or social graphic for an art event, commission offer, or imagined exhibition.

Web

A responsive landing page for a portfolio, workshop, or character-commission service.

Interface

One screen and an interaction storyboard for browsing, booking, saving, or submitting.

Use the critique loop.

First, define the viewer’s job in one sentence. Then make the simplest composition that enables it. Test whether someone can find the purpose and next action; revise one variable at a time. This is the shift from “do I like it?” to “does it communicate?”

Primary source: W3C’s practical visual and interaction design guidance.

Retrieval check

What turns a beautiful first draft into a design that is more likely to work?

Finish with a five-question critique.

  • Can a new viewer say what this is for in five seconds?
  • Do they see the intended first, second, and third read?
  • Are related items closer than unrelated items?
  • Can they read the smallest important text, and find the main action?
  • Does it still work in the narrow version if it is a web piece?

Change the one answer that is weakest, then test again. Keep the rough thumbnail beside the final work: it makes your decision-making visible.

Share the result with your teacher for critique. State the viewer’s job, show the work at its intended size, and ask one question you genuinely want answered. For real-world feedback, use a local AIGA chapter or the Design Buddies community.
