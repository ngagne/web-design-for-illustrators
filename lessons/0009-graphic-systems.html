20–25 minutes · Graphic design

Make a system, not one lucky layout.

A graphic identity becomes useful when it can survive the next format, post, card, or screen without becoming a different design.

Skill: define a compact reusable visual system

Translate what you already know.

From a seriesRecurring palettes, shapes, marks, and compositional habits make individual drawings feel like they inhabit the same world.
In graphic designType roles, colour roles, image crops, spacing, and motifs create an adaptable visual grammar for many pieces of communication.

One idea

Distinguish a system from a template. A template repeats the same arrangement. A system repeats a few decisions while allowing the content and format to change. Decide what must stay stable and what may vary.

Primary source: Material Design’s examples of reusable layout structures across breakpoints.

Retrieval check

Which print concept reminds you that some imagery may extend beyond the trim edge?

Make a mini system sheet.

  1. Name one fixed display type treatment, one body type treatment, three colour roles, and one recurring shape or image-crop rule.
  2. Apply it to a wide poster and a square social card.
  3. Let the format change the composition, but keep the roles recognizable.
  4. Circle the decisions that repeated. Those—not the identical pixels—are your system.

Ask your teacher to review the two versions and name the visual rules they can see. If they cannot name them, strengthen the repeatable decisions.

Remember

Consistency does not mean sameness. It means the same visual rules can answer new needs.
